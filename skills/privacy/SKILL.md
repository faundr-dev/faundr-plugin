---
description: Privacy law for people who don't code. Works out which laws apply from where the app's users are — LGPD (Brazil), GDPR (EU/UK), CCPA/CPRA (California/US), possibly several — then shows what personal data the app collects, which companies it goes to and what is missing under those laws (privacy policy, terms of use, cookie notice, account deletion), and writes the drafts with the owner's OK. Use when the user talks about privacy, LGPD, GDPR, CCPA, terms, cookies, personal data, or before launching.
argument-hint: [policy | terms | cookies | deletion]
allowed-tools: Bash(node "${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/bin/faundr.mjs" *) Bash(faundr *)
---

User request: $ARGUMENTS

1. Run with the Bash tool: `node "${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/bin/faundr.mjs" privacy`. The output lists the personal data collected (and where), the companies that receive data and the status of each item. Check in the code whatever looks odd (e.g. a "cpf" column that actually belongs to a company) and tell the user.
2. **Which laws apply.** It depends on where the app's users are, not where the owner is: **LGPD** for users in Brazil, **GDPR** for the EU (and the UK GDPR for the UK), **CCPA/CPRA** for California (and similar US state laws). Several can apply at once. Look for clues in the code (languages and locales, currency, country field, phone or tax ID formats like CPF/CNPJ, domain, hosting region). If it is not clear, ask the owner one short question (question tool, multiSelect): "Where are your app's users?" with Brazil, EU/UK, United States, Elsewhere. Use the applicable laws in every step below.
3. **Explain in plain language**, without legalese, in a few lines:
   - which laws apply and why;
   - what data the app collects and why (ask why if it is not obvious: "what does the app ask for the date of birth for?"); data that is not used should not be asked for;
   - which companies the data goes to, which ones are outside the users' country or region (international transfer) and which ones are trackers;
   - what is missing, from the most serious to the simplest. Say this does not replace a lawyer, but it is the minimum before opening the app to the public.
4. **Offer to write what is missing**, one at a time, and only write it with the user's OK, in the language of the app's users. Before that, ask in a single round (user question tool) what the code does not say: company or controller name, tax ID (CNPJ/CPF in Brazil; company number elsewhere), email for privacy requests (LGPD encarregado/DPO, GDPR contact — it can be the owner in a small app; GDPR may also need an EU/UK representative if the company is outside them), city or jurisdiction for disputes, and how long data is kept after the account is deleted.
   - **Privacy policy**: a page in the app (follow the project's routes and DESIGN.md), with: who the controller is and the contact; what data it collects and for what (the list from step 1, with the legal basis for each use under LGPD/GDPR: performance of a contract, consent, legal obligation or legitimate interest; under CCPA/CPRA, the categories collected and whether data is sold or shared for advertising); who it shares with (the companies from step 1, saying which are outside the users' country or region); how long it keeps data; the person's rights under each applicable law (confirm, access, correct, delete, port the data, withdraw consent, object; under CCPA/CPRA, know, delete, correct, opt out of sale/sharing — with a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link if it sells or shares — and no discrimination) and how to request them; cookies; security; date of last update. Sensitive data or children's data, if any, with specific consent (LGPD/GDPR) or opt-in (CCPA/CPRA).
   - **Terms of use**: a page with what the app offers, usage rules, account and password, payments and cancellation (if any), liability limits, changes to the terms and jurisdiction.
   - **Cookie notice**: only if there is a tracker with cookies. A notice with "Accept" and "Reject" (rejecting as easy as accepting) that only loads the trackers after acceptance, and saves the choice. Under CCPA/CPRA, also honor the Global Privacy Control signal as an opt-out. Prefer a small, maintained library (e.g. vanilla-cookieconsent) and ask for the OK before installing.
   - **Delete the account**: a button in Account/Settings that asks for confirmation and deletes the person's data (or anonymizes what the law requires keeping, like invoices), including in the login service.
   - Link the pages in the footer or at sign-up ("By creating an account you accept the Terms and the Privacy Policy").
5. After writing, run `node "${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/bin/faundr.mjs" privacy` again to check and say what changed. The result appears in the dashboard under **Launch → LGPD**.
